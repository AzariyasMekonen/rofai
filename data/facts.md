# Selam Children's Village: key facts (verified from the school's own magazines)

Selam Children's Village (SCV) was founded in July 1986 in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia, by Mrs. Tsehay Roeschli (also spelled Röschli). She started with 28 orphans and 9 workers. The first orphans had lost their parents in the drought and famine in the northern regions of Ethiopia; later many children became orphans through HIV/AIDS. 2026 is the school's 40th anniversary.

Founder's story: David and Marie-Luise Roeschli, a Swiss couple, lived with their four children on the outskirts of Addis Ababa, running a chicken farm. They adopted Tsehay and her five younger sisters and brothers, who were their neighbours. Later, Tsehay visited her native Ethiopia during a severe famine. Shocked, she returned to Switzerland and decided to build an orphanage in Addis Ababa for 28 children.

Founding timeline:
- 1985: The association "Verein Kinderheim Selam" was founded in Switzerland. Tsehay moved to Addis Ababa. The government granted 30,000 m2 of land. In May the first containers were shipped from Switzerland.
- 1986: On 16 January the foundation stone of the main building was laid. On 16 July 1986, 19 boys and 13 girls moved in; 37 more children came during the year.
- 1987: David and Marie-Luise Roeschli moved to Selam. The government granted 400,000 m2 of land 3 km from the orphanage for a Technical and Vocational Center. Selam Elementary School was established in 1987, starting with 54 boys and 55 girls.
- 1994: Selam High School was established, starting with 57 grade 9 students.
- 1996: Selam Girls' Vocational Center was established.
- Selam Children's Village II: the first house was inaugurated by Ethiopian President Girma Wolde Giorgis with the Swiss Ambassador Dr. René Schätti; 31 children moved in.

Leadership: Mr. David Roeschli served as Managing Director of Selam Children's Village and of the Selam Technical and Vocational Center (STVC).

What Selam does: it raises orphaned and needy children in family-style homes and gives them food, clothing, health care, schooling (elementary and high school), and vocational training (Selam Technical and Vocational College, Girls' Vocational Center). It also runs farms (dairy, fish, rabbit, bee, vegetable, flower), supports community members, and trained prison inmates at the Tesfa skill training center. Selam Business Group PLC, a production and training center near Awasa, was started with former Selam trainees.
